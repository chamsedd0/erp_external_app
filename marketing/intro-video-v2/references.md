# References — what was actually viewed

Reviewed 28 September 2026. How each source was reviewed is stated per entry. Nothing here claims a
continuous audiovisual watch-through, and no reference audio was listened to.

## 1. Introducing iPhone Air — official Apple Canada upload

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeoJC9Thl9I> (runtime 2:29.7)

**Method:** viewed in a browser. The player was paused and seeked, and frames were drawn from it:
every 5 s from 0:02 to 2:27, every 1 s from 0:00 to 0:11, and single frames at 0:40, 0:41.5, 0:43,
0:44.5, 0:46, 0:47.5, 2:10, 2:14, 2:18, 2:21, 2:23 and 2:26. Audio was muted.

### Observation A — one attribute, one slow move (0:00–0:11)
- **Seen:** an extreme, edge-on close-up of the device's side against a pale grey-blue studio lit by
  soft vertical window shadows. The camera travels slowly and continuously down the edge, past the
  side button. The only text is a small lower-third name caption, present from about 0:04. At about
  0:09–0:10 a hand enters from below and holds the phone. The fingers give scale, which makes the
  thinness legible without a headline.
- **What it accomplishes:** it defines a single product attribute through silhouette, light and a
  human-scale reference. The move is slow enough to read, and nothing else competes for attention.
- **Shadow Portal interpretation (shot 01):** one controlled move along the device's upper edge. The
  attribute we define is "real, readable company software in an employee's hand", so the dashboard
  must be identifiable by 0.60 s. A 20-second film can't hold a silhouette for nine seconds, so we
  compress the idea rather than copy it. The portal appears as a shaped reflection travelling across
  the glass, not as a graphic overlay.

### Observation B — a single lit opening as the frame (0:41.5–0:46)
- **Seen:** at 0:41.5, a dark room with one bright rectangular doorway and a silhouetted figure. From
  0:43 to 0:46, the same rectangle of light seen from above becomes a frame holding four devices on a
  dark floor.
- **What it accomplishes:** the frame is a motivated light source, not decoration. It isolates the
  product and gives the scene a graphic shape. It appears for one idea, then the film moves on.
- **Shadow Portal interpretation (shots 02 and 06):** our opening is the arch. In the hero shot it is
  a real light behind the phone. That light is the motivated source of the blue rim on the device
  edges, and it frames the product once. In the final shot the same silhouette resolves into the
  mark. This replaces v1's tunnel, which repeated behind every scene.

### Observation C — the ending rests (2:14–2:26)
- **Seen:** at 2:14 the device stands edge-on and small in a large pale field. At 2:18 a hand holds it
  edge-on. At 2:21 three silhouettes stand side by side. At 2:23 a white card shows only the logo,
  centred and small. At 2:26 the frame is black.
- **What it accomplishes:** stillness and negative space let the final product state and the logo
  read with no competing motion. The logo holds alone.
- **Shadow Portal interpretation (shot 06):** every arrival completes by 17.00 s. The lockup then holds
  for three full seconds with no flares, sweeps or last-second reveals. Unlike the reference, we hold
  the lockup to the final frame instead of cutting to black, as the brief asks.

## 2. Meet Liquid Glass — WWDC25 session 219

<https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/>

**Method:** official page transcript read in a browser. The timecodes come from the transcript
markup (`data-start`). The video itself was not watched.

- **6:37** — "shadows become more prominent to create additional separation": separation grows with
  what sits underneath.
- **13:07** — "best reserved for the navigation layer": the material serves content and doesn't
  replace it.
- **13:24** — "always avoid glass on glass".
- **17:21–17:29** — "Tinting should only be used to bring emphasis … Avoid tinting all your elements."
- **Shadow Portal interpretation:** blue is reserved for identity and one motivated light accent.
  Proof shots sit in a neutral pale studio, where the app's own UI is the content. Film annotations
  are not stacked over the UI. We do not dress the real app in an unimplemented glass redesign.

## 3. Designing Fluid Interfaces — WWDC18 session 803

<https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/>

**Method:** official page transcript read in a browser, with the timecodes it displays. The video
itself was not watched.

- **5:33–5:37** — "that begins with response … people are really, really sensitive to latency."
- **11:55–12:11** — "maintain spatial consistency throughout movement … things smoothly leave and
  enter our perception in symmetric paths."
- **34:33–34:52** — damping controls overshoot, "from 100% damping, where there will be no
  overshoot", and response controls "how quickly the value will try and get to the target".
- **Shadow Portal interpretation:**
  - The Submit press responds within 6–10 frames.
  - Magnified UI details grow out of, and return to, their source position.
  - The device, the typography and the status detail do not share one spring. The device lands
    critically damped, with no overshoot. Only the small status arrival gets near-critical damping.
